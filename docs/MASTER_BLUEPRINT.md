# Space Hippie — Master Production Blueprint
**Version:** 0.1 | **Status:** Pre-production; engine and vertical slice await owner approval.

## High concept
A stylized polygonal single-player open-world science-fiction crime sandbox set in a scruffy backwater where reality has started leaking. The player begins broke, with a cheap ride and a small hideout, then builds a livelihood through honest jobs, exploration, trading, alien resource gathering, fictional contraband crafting, risky deliveries, and relationships. The town and its residents react to the player's choices.

## Creative north star
**“Roadside Americana after the universe developed a strange leak.”** The town feels practical, sun-faded, and lived-in. Alternate dimensions feel beautiful, vivid, and subtly impossible. The tone is oddball crime comedy with melancholy and occasional cosmic unease.

## Pillars
1. Dense over vast: every block contains a useful place, story, shortcut, character, or visual joke.
2. Strange but grounded: normal-world needs contrast with uncanny dimensions.
3. Freedom with legible consequences: players understand why NPCs and authorities react.
4. Systems create stories: missions recombine reusable systems.
5. Style is a feature: silhouettes, palette, signs, sound, lighting, and animation are recognizable.
6. Polished scope beats feature count.
7. Offline-first single-player; no developer-operated server needed.

## Provisional canon
- **Town:** Dusty Junction, a failed freight stop on the fictional moonlet Lumen-9, in a neglected system locals call the Fringe.
- **First dimension:** The Bloom, a twilight pocket where luminous plants grow around mineral fractures.
- **Tone:** funny without constant jokes; crime is a choice with consequences, not the only viable route.
Names are proposals and can be changed through the decision log before production.

## Player promise
Within 30 minutes, the player should learn movement, meet two memorable residents, discover a portal, gather an alien resource, return and sell or process it, earn a meaningful upgrade, and see a legible consequence for a risky choice.

## First playable scope
One compact town district, hideout, portal, one dimension, one player character, one drivable vehicle, interaction, inventory/currency, one resource activity, one processing/sale choice, one complete mission, basic save/load, controller foundations, and one simple suspicion response.

Explicitly deferred: multiple towns/dimensions, seamless streaming, multiplayer, persistent public servers, traffic simulation, destructible buildings, sprawling skill trees, full faction warfare, procedural city generation, and deep survival meters.

## First-session arc
1. Wake in a cheap converted motel room with little money.
2. Meet mechanic Mara Venn; accept a courier lead.
3. Drive to the abandoned Pumpworks utility site.
4. Discover the portal and travel to The Bloom.
5. Gather three Lumen Pods; optionally risk a rare specimen.
6. Return safely; sell raw pods for low risk or process them into Glow Resin for higher value and more suspicion.
7. Resolve a simple witness/enforcement encounter if cargo is exposed.
8. Buy a storage upgrade or repair; unlock a lead about a second portal signature.

## Progression principle
Unlock verbs and options, not only statistics. Storage expands haul capacity; insulated gloves allow hazardous collection; suspension opens rough routes; contacts reveal buyers/jobs; reputation affects access; processing upgrades change yield. No irreversible morality lock, though important choices are remembered.

## Production constraints
PC/Linux is the first development target. PlayStation/Xbox are commercial goals, subject to SDK access, platform-holder approval, porting resources, testing, and certification. Engine remains open pending evidence-based UE5/Godot evaluation. C++-first is a provisional workflow preference, not a mandate to fight an engine's natural architecture.

## Success
The world is enjoyable to explore; movement/driving are responsive; the visual identity is coherent; portal travel matters; the resource-to-profit loop is satisfying; consequences are understandable; progression changes options; the codebase is maintainable; the first build is polished rather than overloaded.
