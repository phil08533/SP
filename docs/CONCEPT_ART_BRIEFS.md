# Concept Art Briefs and Visual Reference Targets

These briefs describe the images the project should commission or generate for visual exploration. They are not final assets and do not authorize bulk generation. Use the same palette and silhouette rules across the set. Avoid copying a specific existing game's characters, logos, or exact environments.

## Board A — The town at dusk
**Prompt direction:** wide 16:9 environment concept of a compact, low-poly retro-futurist roadside town on a remote moonlet; sun-faded teal motel, crooked utility poles and cables, small garage, diner canopy, oxidized signs, dusty asphalt, strange violet glow behind an abandoned pumping station; stylized chunky forms, intentional composition, selective saturated accents, cinematic but readable third-person game scale.  
**Must answer:** what is the skyline silhouette, where is the portal landmark, how do town colors feel distinct from alien colors, and what makes this place memorable at thumbnail size?

## Board B — The player hideout
**Prompt direction:** small converted motel service room, lived-in but not grimdark, cheap bed, battered fridge, hand-drawn map, storage crate, makeshift workbench, violet vacancy light entering through blinds, stylized polygonal props, warm practical lamp and cool outdoor spill.  
**Must answer:** can a few visual changes communicate progression? Is the room practical and cozy enough to be a repeatable hub?

## Board C — Starter vehicle
**Prompt direction:** three-quarter front and side views of a battered compact retro-futurist road car, chunky readable silhouette, oversized lamps, repaired body panels, thick tires, practical bumper, faded turquoise with rust and cream accents; game-ready proportions, no brand logos, no excessive greebles.  
**Must answer:** can the player recognize it from far away? Does it look driveable and mechanically coherent? Are wheel placement and ground clearance clear?

## Board D — Character lineup
**Prompt direction:** neutral full-body turnaround sheet of five stylized town residents: pragmatic mechanic Mara, eccentric pattern spotter Finch, diner owner Jo, measured officer Vale, transactional broker Rook. Shared proportions and material language, varied silhouettes and clothing shapes, expressive faces without realism overload, plain background, front/side/three-quarter views.  
**Must answer:** can each character be identified in silhouette alone? Are their jobs readable without costume clichés?

## Board E — Portal at Pumpworks
**Prompt direction:** municipal industrial chamber with a vertical liquid-glass tear held by repurposed metal braces, faded safety stripes, utility cables, scorched concrete, a subtle impossible shadow, dust caught in a soft harmonic glow. The portal should look like an infrastructure accident, not a fantasy gate.  
**Must answer:** is the interaction target readable, is the frame modular, can the effect be achieved without a particle-heavy performance cost?

## Board F — The Bloom environment
**Prompt direction:** compact alien pocket ecosystem at twilight, huge layered luminous plants, faceted mineral seams, root bridge over dark mineral pool, indigo and petrol shadows, mint/lime emissive accents, amber return arch, restrained coral details, generous dark negative space, stylized low-poly game environment, readable path.  
**Must answer:** where does the eye travel, can the return arch be found, is the environment beautiful before it is dangerous, and are the collectibles visually distinct from decoration?

## Board G — UI language
**Prompt direction:** fictional retro utility interface with near-black blue-gray panels, warm off-white text, muted teal focus, amber warnings, restrained violet anomaly indicators, chunky icons, readable controller focus, minimal clutter.  
**Must answer:** can objective, inventory, currency, and suspicion be understood quickly? No state should rely on color alone.

## Board H — Lighting comparison
Produce the same town corner and the same alien grove in three lighting passes: neutral readability, cinematic mood, and performance-conscious game lighting. Compare silhouette clarity, fog, emissive intensity, shadow stability, and material response. The production choice must work in motion, not only in a still image.

## Review method
For each board, rate: identity, readability, cohesion, production feasibility, and performance risk. Keep rejected versions and record why they failed. Approve one target before making a large asset family.
